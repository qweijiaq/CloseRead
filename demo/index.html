The Quiet Cost of Cheap Attention

精读导读

早上醒来第一件事，你是不是也摸过手机？还没跟任何人说话，注意力已经先交给了一家你永远不会见面的公司。这笔交易看起来免费，其实不免费。今天我们就借一段关于“注意力经济”的文字，学10个能用在写作和口语里的表达，顺便想清楚一个问题：我们的注意力，到底被谁拿走了。

逐段精读

01

Every morning, millions of people wake up and reach for a small rectangle of glass. Before they have spoken to another person, they have already given a piece of their attention to a company they will never meet. The exchange feels free. It is not.

每天早晨，数百万人醒来后伸手去拿一块小长方形玻璃。在他们与另一个人交谈之前，他们已经将一部分注意力给了一家他们永远不会见面的公司。这种交换感觉是免费的。但事实并非如此。

02

Attention has become one of the defining commodities of the modern economy. Unlike oil or wheat, it cannot be stored, shipped, or saved for later. It is scarce, personal, and perishable. The platforms that capture it rarely sell it directly; they sell access to the people whose attention they have collected. Advertisers pay for the chance to interrupt a moment that might otherwise have belonged to the reader.

注意力已成为现代经济中具有定义性的大宗商品之一。与石油或小麦不同，它不能被储存、运输或留待以后使用。它是稀缺的、个人的、易逝的。获取注意力的平台很少直接出售它；它们出售的是接触那些注意力被收集的人的机会。广告商付费购买打断某个时刻的机会，而这个时刻原本可能属于读者。

03

The economics are simple; the consequences are not. When a resource is abundant, companies compete on quality. When attention is scarce, they compete on capture. The result is a landscape designed to produce compulsion rather than satisfaction: endless feeds, autoplay, notifications, streaks, and small rewards delivered at unpredictable intervals. Each feature is rational for the platform. Together, they can undermine a person's ability to sustain deliberate thought.

经济学很简单；后果却不简单。当资源丰富时，公司竞争质量。当注意力稀缺时，他们竞争捕获。结果是一个旨在产生强迫而非满足的景观：无尽的信息流、自动播放、通知、连续记录以及以不可预测的间隔提供的小奖励。每个功能对平台来说都是理性的。它们合在一起，会削弱一个人维持深思熟虑的能力。

04

Fragmentation is the hidden tax. A task that once required an hour of quiet now arrives in a dozen pieces, each one separated by a notification, a headline, or a short video. The mind adapts to the rhythm of interruption, and the adaptation is almost invisible. People do not notice that they have stopped reading long sentences; they simply begin to avoid them. The loss is not only time. It is the capacity to follow an argument from beginning to end.

碎片化是隐藏的税。曾经需要一小时安静完成的任务现在分成十几块，每一块都被通知、标题或短视频隔开。思维适应了中断的节奏，而这种适应几乎看不见。人们没有注意到他们已经停止阅读长句子；他们只是开始避免它们。损失不仅是时间。而是从头到尾跟随一个论点的能力。

05

The word "friction" has become a villain in product design. A little friction, however, is what turns an impulse into a decision. Making it slightly harder to open an app, or slightly easier to close it, can restore a measure of autonomy. Some companies have begun to sell subscription products precisely because they do not need to interrupt their users. Their incentive is to make the experience durable rather than addictive.

“摩擦”一词在产品设计中已成为反派。然而，一点摩擦正是将冲动转化为决策的关键。让打开应用稍微难一点，或让关闭它稍微容易一点，可以恢复一定程度的自主权。一些公司开始销售订阅产品，正是因为他们不需要打断用户。他们的动机是让体验持久而非上瘾。

06

Critics of this view sometimes argue that people should simply pay attention better. But attention is not a muscle that grows through scolding. It is a skill shaped by the environment. Children who grow up in a room full of interruptions learn a different relationship to silence than children who do not. Adults who spend eight hours a day responding to alerts become good at responding and worse at staying. The damage is gradual, which is why it is easy to dismiss.

这种观点的批评者有时认为，人们应该更好地集中注意力。但注意力不是通过责骂就能增长的肌肉。它是一种由环境塑造的技能。在充满干扰的房间里长大的孩子，与没有这种经历的孩子相比，对沉默的关系不同。每天花八小时回应警报的成年人变得善于回应，却更不善于停留。损害是渐进的，这就是为什么它容易被忽视。

07

This is not an argument that attention is a zero-sum game. A well-made documentary, a difficult novel, or a thoughtful conversation can leave a person more alert than before. The question is who gets to choose the direction of that alertness. When every available surface is optimized for extraction, the burden of curation falls on the individual. That is a heavy job, and most people are already tired.

这并不是说注意力是零和游戏。一部制作精良的纪录片、一本难懂的小说或一次深思熟虑的对话可以让人比之前更警觉。问题是，谁来选择这种警觉的方向。当每一个可用的表面都被优化用于提取时，策展的负担就落在了个人身上。这是一项繁重的工作，而大多数人已经累了。

08

The most promising response may be neither abstinence nor willpower but design. Tools that protect a block of time, screens that stay quiet until asked, and products that charge for use instead of attention all point in the same direction. They treat attention as something to be spent wisely, not something to be harvested.

09

The cost of cheap attention is not always visible on a bill. It appears in the book you did not finish, the conversation you did not hear, and the idea that never had room to develop. Reclaiming that space is not a matter of discipline alone. It is a matter of choosing, again and again, what deserves to be noticed.

廉价注意力的成本并不总是体现在账单上。它出现在你没有读完的书、你没有听到的对话，以及从未有空间发展的想法中。重新夺回那个空间不仅仅是纪律的问题。这是一个一次又一次选择什么值得被注意的问题。

完形填空

  1. Attention has become one of the defining ______ of the modern economy.

  4. Together, they can ______ a person's ability to sustain deliberate thought.

    答案：Fragmentation — the process of breaking into small pieces or parts

  6. Making it slightly harder to open an app, or slightly easier to close it, can restore a measure of ______.

    答案：autonomy — the ability to make your own decisions

  7. Their ______ is to make the experience durable rather than addictive.

    答案：incentive — something that motivates or encourages someone to do something

  8. When every available surface is optimized for extraction, the burden of ______ falls on the individual.

    答案：curation — the process of carefully selecting and organizing content

  9. The most promising response may be neither ______ nor willpower but design.

    答案：abstinence — the practice of not doing something you enjoy, especially drinking alcohol

阅读理解

  1. Why does the author say that attention is unlike oil or wheat?

    答案：The author says attention cannot be stored, shipped, or saved for later; it is scarce, personal, and perishable.

  2. According to the text, what do platforms actually sell to advertisers?

    答案：Platforms sell access to the people whose attention they have collected, not attention itself.

  3. What is the 'hidden tax' mentioned in the text, and what does it cost people?

    答案：Fragmentation is the hidden tax; it costs people not only time but also the capacity to follow an argument from beginning to end.

  4. How does the author suggest that a little friction can be beneficial?

    答案：A little friction turns an impulse into a decision and can restore a measure of autonomy, for example by making it slightly harder to open an app or easier to close it.

  5. What counterargument do critics of this view make, and how does the author respond?

    答案：Critics argue that people should simply pay attention better; the author responds that attention is not a muscle that grows through scolding but a skill shaped by the environment.

  6. What does the author propose as the most promising response to the problem of cheap attention?

    答案：The author proposes design—tools that protect a block of time, screens that stay quiet until asked, and products that charge for use instead of attention—rather than abstinence or willpower.

讨论 / 输出

  1. Do you agree that attention is a commodity? Why or why not?

    答案：I agree because attention is scarce and valuable, and companies pay to capture it. However, unlike traditional commodities, attention is deeply personal and cannot be transferred like a product.

  2. Have you experienced fragmentation in your own daily life? Give an example.

    答案：Yes, I often find myself switching between tasks because of notifications. For instance, while reading, I check my phone every few minutes, which breaks my concentration.

  3. What small design changes could help you protect your attention?

    答案：Turning off non-essential notifications and using apps that limit screen time could help. Also, keeping my phone in another room while working would reduce interruptions.

  4. Is abstinence from technology a realistic solution? Why or why not?

    答案：Complete abstinence is unrealistic for most people because technology is integrated into work and social life. Instead, moderation and intentional use are more practical.

  5. Who should be responsible for curating attention—individuals, platforms, or governments?

    答案：All three share responsibility: individuals must make conscious choices, platforms should design ethically, and governments can regulate addictive practices. A combined effort is needed.
